Final Table Awaits After a Commanding Serbian Run

The Open Catan Championship in Prague reaches its decisive day, with Serbia's Milan Kajganovic one victory away from claiming the title of the best Catanist.

By the Competition Desk · Published October 4, 2026 · Prague, Czech Republic

Yesterday's qualification rounds set the tone for a demanding and sharply contested Open Catan Championship in Prague. Across six games, the field was tested in negotiation, timing, resource discipline and the ability to read a shifting board under pressure.

Among the standout performances was that of Serbian representative Milan Kajganovic, who produced six highly respectable games and converted three of them into victories. In a tournament format where consistency often matters as much as a single dominant result, Kajganovic's qualification record has placed him firmly in the conversation for the championship crown.

Today is the final day of competition. The remaining players now enter the decisive stage with one clear objective: the title of the best Catanist. For Kajganovic, the equation is direct and unforgiving. One final game remains, and victory in that match would secure the title.

Prague has become the centre of the Catan world this weekend, with every settlement, road and development card carrying the weight of a championship. The final table promises the kind of tactical tension that defines top-level Catan: calculated risk, precise trading, controlled expansion and composure when the dice refuse to cooperate.

All eyes now turn to the final board. After three wins from six impressive qualification games, Milan Kajganovic stands on the edge of a historic Serbian result. The title is within reach, but in Catan, it must still be earned one turn at a time.
